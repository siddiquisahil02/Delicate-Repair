Therapy Approaches / IFS

Internal Family Systems (IFS)

A paradigm shift in how we understand the mind — and a path toward genuine self-leadership.

What is Internal Family Systems?

Internal Family Systems (IFS) is a powerful therapeutic approach often used in the treatment of complex trauma (C-PTSD), dissociation, and dissociative disorders. IFS is both a theory and a psychotherapy model, and many consider it a paradigm shift in how we understand the mind.

IFS proposes that each of us has an internal system made up of different parts. These parts can be understood as subpersonalities or inner figures that develop to help us navigate the world. Each part takes on specific roles in an effort to protect us and help us survive.

Have You Ever Said This to Yourself?

"A part of me wants to move forward, but another part of me is holding back."

Most of us have experienced this internally. At times, we may notice ourselves shifting between different parts depending on the situation or emotional state — the part that withdraws, the one that rages, the inner critic, the helper who gives too much.

The Core Principle: No Bad Parts

Through Internal Family Systems, you learn to identify, listen to, and develop compassion for your parts. A core principle of IFS is that there are no bad parts. Every part has a protective intention, even if its strategies feel disruptive or extreme.

The parts that cause us the most pain — the saboteur, the perfectionist, the shutdown part — are often the most desperate for understanding. IFS gives us a way to approach them with curiosity rather than judgment.

What IFS Can Help With

  • C-PTSD and complex trauma
  • Dissociation
  • Eating disorders
  • Addiction
  • Inner conflict and ambivalence
  • Shame and self-criticism
  • Relationship patterns
  • Identity confusion
  • Depression
  • Anxiety

What to Expect in IFS Sessions

IFS sessions involve a lot of curiosity and internal exploration. You won't be pushed to "fix" your parts or make them go away. Instead, we get to know them — what they carry, what they're protecting, and what they need.

By understanding and working with your internal system in this way, you can gain clarity about your symptoms, patterns, and inner conflicts — and begin moving toward greater internal balance and self-leadership.
